## 1. What is now certain

**EML 63 (Huang et al., PIML substructures)**

- The condensed operator is a learned output. §3.1: "two feedforward neural networks are respectively used to predict the elements of N_j^h and K-tilde_j^h ... by using its nature as a symmetric matrix, only the upper triangular array part of K-tilde_j^h needs to be learned."
- The network input is modulus, never geometry. §3.1: "Young's modulus of the elements in the substructure is used as the input parameter of the feedforward neural network, while Poisson's ratio is set as 0.3."
- The learned 3D trace is 24 DOF. §3.2: "can be further reduced to N n_ji × 18 and 171 under the substructure discretized with the eight-node brick element." (19×18/2 = 171, 24−6 = 18.) All three §4 examples are 3D, so nothing wider is learned in any reported run.
- Symmetry and the 6-dimensional rigid kernel hold **exactly, by construction of the parameterisation** — not post hoc. §3.3: "the outputs of the neural networks are the remaining part of N_j^h and K-tilde_j^h after removing the invariants ... it is necessary to complement the N_j2 and K-tilde_j^h based on the outputs of the neural network according to the Eqs. (13)-(15)." Which 171 entries are independent is never stated anywhere, so the targets are pivot-basis-dependent in an unstated way.
- No spectral vocabulary exists in the paper. Exhaustive grep: `definite`, `positive`, `eigen`, `spectr`, `factoriz`, `cholesky`, `square root`, `Schur`, `Steklov` — **zero occurrences combined**.
- No sensitivity equation appears. `sensitiv` occurs twice, both in prose; `adjoint` twice; no derivative of any operator with respect to any variable is written down. §4: "the optimality criteria (OC) method is used to update design variables."
- The only accuracy number in the paper is 7.56% / 7.65% displacement error at one output point, §4.2, and the baseline is EMsFEM — the same corner-reduced model solved exactly — so the linear-trace modelling error is excluded from it. No compliance error, no operator-norm error, no held-out error, no loss value, no train/test split.
- **The headline speedup is contradicted by the paper's own measurements.** Abstract: "it can achieve 10^4–10^5 times solution efficiency." §4.1: "about several hundred times (for NF=164,7750, tt/tPIML=472; for NF=11,718,750, tt/tPIML=328)" — and the ratio *falls* with refinement. The baseline is degraded further: "this paper only reports the average time of the first 10 iterations and we have not obtained the final optimized results" and "due to the limitation of our computer storage, the traditional SIMP method was not applied for the model with NF=37,989,750." §4.3 reports no baseline at all.
- **Their own experiment publishes a negative result for direct operator prediction.** §4.2, Fig. 9 compares the Eq. (17) route against the predicted operator: "neural networks can reduce the time for calculating the condensed stiffness matrix by over 60%" but "much more details and smoother boundary are observed in the result of Fig. 9(a), and the value of U_out^EMs in Fig. 9(b) is smaller, which implies a poor performance."
- Zero interior load is hard-coded. Eq. (6): "it is assumed that the external load pertaining to u_ji^h is zero without loss of generality."
- Training distribution is i.i.d. uniform: "the training samples are generated in the range of [0,1] through a random process." Deployment is filtered, near-binary SIMP fields with Emin = 10^-7, patched by a ρ_v = 10^-4 bypass. The SIMP exponent p is defined ("p denoting a penalization parameter") and never given a value.

**CAD 193 (Chen & Li, conditioned numerical shape functions)**

- No network exists in the method; Algorithm 1 is 15 algebraic steps.
- **Section 6's first future-work item is our thesis, verbatim:** "while the computation of numerical shape functions incurs a small cost per coarse element, it becomes significant when dealing with a large number of coarse elements. Its efficiency can be improved with the aid of deep neural networks or parallel computation techniques [29,30,44]."
- The paper identifies our object, names it, and cites it. Eq. (A.11): "K_α = (Ψ^α)^T S^α Ψ^α", with "S^α = k_bb − k_ib^T k_ii^{-1} k_ib ... The matrix S^α is the Schur complement [45] of the block." Their element operator *is* the full-trace Steklov–Poincaré operator compressed by a polynomial interpolator, and their entire conditioning theory is a theory of how that compression degenerates.
- The emitted operator is the coarse one, not the trace one. Eq. (34) drives the interior solves with "fbar = −k_ib Ψ_α", so the solve count is the coarse DOF count. Per-element coarse size is derived from Appendix B's "n_F = (p+1)^2" at p = 3: 4³ − 2³ = 56 nodes = **168 DOF**, corroborated by Table 2 (derived 47.3K vs reported 44.5K on mesh-1; 106.7K vs 97.6K on mesh-2, the gap being removed ill-conditioned nodes).
- Symmetry and PSD are free, never enforced. Eq. (17): "K_α = (p_α)^T k_f p_α"; §4.3 then merely asserts "where K_α is a symmetric positive semi-definite matrix."
- **They already separate the per-element rigid nullspace.** §4.3: "The zero eigenvalues that correspond to rigid body modes (translations and rotations) should be extracted from V_0, in order to avoid non-physical behavior."
- **They already have a graded, corner-driven density field.** §3.2 assumption (2): "When a fine element in D_h,α is cut, its material density is taken as the average of the material densities at its corner nodes, where the nodal density is assigned as 1 or 0 based on whether the node is located inside or outside the domain."
- No group structure. Grep: `equivarian` 0, `invarian` 0, `group` 0; `symmetr` 2 hits, both matrix symmetry. Appendix A.4's "the dimension index k is removed by using the Frobenius norm because of the isotropy of Ψ_I across different dimensions" is index bookkeeping, not a geometric statement.
- No optimisation of any kind. Grep: `sensitiv` 0, `adjoint` 0, `compliance` 0; `optimiz` 3 hits, all reference titles.
- Their conditioning result **assumes away the fine-scale cut pathology**. Theorem 2: "Suppose that the substructure problem in Eq. (6) for each coarse element D_α is well-conditioned, as discussed in Section 3.2", secured by voxel density averaging plus a fallback: "an extra soft material can also be introduced to avoid an ill-conditioned substructure problem, as conducted in previous works [5,8]."
- Their trace is not fixed-dimension and their element is not local. §4.1: "b = {xbar_1, ..., xbar_{n_b}} denotes the retained boundary fine node set" and "Notation Ψ_I, without the superscript α, is also used to refer to the assembled matrix from all coarse elements Ψ_I^α incident at coarse node I" — the keep/drop decision for a coarse node depends on neighbours.
- Scope limits: "In this study, planar Dirichlet boundary conditions are assumed for easy enforcement in strong form"; condition numbers are estimated — "approximated using the MATLAB function condest."

## 2. What these two papers take from us

**The idea is not ours.** Learning a per-cell condensed operator and driving topology optimisation with it is published (EML 63). Proposing a deep network to replace per-coarse-element numerical shape-function computation is published (CAD 193, Section 6, first item). Learning a 3D microstructure-to-property map with a CNN is published by CAD 193's own last author (ref [44], Zhu, Wang, Zhong, Li, *Vis Comput* 2024). CAD 193's Related Work states the connection explicitly: "Recently, Huang et al. also proposed the problem-independent machine learning (PIML) approach to establish a mapping from a heterogeneous cell element to its numerical shape functions through deep neural networks [29,30]." The Zhejiang group holds the numerical baseline, the learner, and the stated intent to combine them. They are the competitor and they are one paper away.

**Geometry as network input is not ours.** EML 63's Conclusions (3): "an ML model can be established to predict the relationship between the stiffness of an isogeometric element and the coordinates of its control points ... with the use of the techniques developed in the present work." Continuous geometric parameters as network input are anticipated in words. So is the motivation, verbatim: "using fixed structured grids by embedding the complex physical domain in them and therefore totally circumventing the tedious mesh generation process."

**The rigid-body quotient is not ours.** EML 63 enforces K-tilde φ = 0 exactly by parameterising only the independent entries; CAD 193 extracts rigid translations and rotations per element by eigendecomposition. Both isolate the 6-dimensional rigid trace of the per-cell operator. Our contribution there is narrow: a *fixed, pivot-basis-independent* Householder quotient instead of a per-element numerical eigen-split with a tolerance.

**A graded corner-driven material field is not ours.** CAD 193 already averages 0/1 corner densities per cut fine element.

**The free exact gradient is the one that hurts.** For the static condensation N = [I; −K_ii^{-1} K_ib] one has K N = [K-tilde; 0] and dN has a zero top block, so the dN terms in d(N^T K N) cancel identically and d(K-tilde)/dρ = N^T (dK/dρ) N holds exactly, for one extra triple product with an analytically known dK_e/dρ_e. EML 63's own trusted Eq. (17) route therefore hands it exact operator sensitivities without differentiating any network. Our d(operator)/dτ gate does not fill a hole in the literature; it patches a hole we create by predicting the operator directly. A reviewer who knows the cancellation will read it that way unless we say it first.

**The cost bar is theirs and we are on the wrong side of it.** CAD 193 Table 2: shape functions take 15.6 s over a 15×15×3 coarse mesh (675 elements) and 31.1 s over 20×20×4 (1600 elements) — **23 ms and 19 ms per coarse element**, 3D elasticity, 10³ local fine mesh, p = 3, C++/Eigen/TBB on an i7-11700; §5.6's 183-million-fine-node run is "523.0 s" over 60×40×12 = 28,800 elements, **18 ms per element for the whole pipeline**. EML 63 §4.3: 1.024 million substructures at "the average time of the one iteration step is equal to 5677.8 s", i.e. **5.5 ms per substructure for an entire optimisation iteration**, of which OC alone "still takes about 26% of the total time." Our 2 s per cell is 90–360× the measured classical cost. "The classical route is too slow" is not defensible on either paper's numbers.

## 3. What they leave, with the number that proves it

- **Output width.** EML 63 learns 171 independent entries on a 24-DOF trace; CAD 193 emits 168 DOF per hexahedron at p = 3 (from "n_F = (p+1)^2"). Our q ≈ 20,000 is ~830× EML 63's trace and ~120× CAD 193's. Neither paper prints its own trace size; CAD 193's uncut fine-boundary bound is 602 nodes / 1806 DOF at 10³, and for cut cells it is smaller and configuration-dependent — their trace is not even a fixed-dimension object.
- **PSD and a factored operator.** EML 63: `definite`, `positive`, `eigen`, `factoriz`, `square root` = **0 occurrences**. CAD 193 gets SPSD free from the congruence K_α = p_α^T k_f p_α and asserts it without proof. Nothing in either paper addresses definiteness of a *predicted* operator. EML 63's largest result is the most exposed: §4.3 solves the condensed system iteratively — "the linear equations on coarse-resolution mesh are solved with the iterative method" — with no preconditioner, no iteration count, and no definiteness safeguard reported. M_q = B^T A^{-1/2} B has no counterpart at the level of vocabulary.
- **Discrete-group covariance on cut cells.** CAD 193: `equivarian` 0, `invarian` 0, `group` 0. EML 63: `equivarian` 0, `augment` 0, `permut` 0; `invarian` 1 hit, meaning "the invariants (i.e., N_j1)". EML 63's Eq. (14) constrains infinitesimal rotation of the *shape functions*, not covariance of the operator under relabelling. Our 1e-14 in a pivot-invariant nodal pullback is unmatched, and note that i.i.d. uniform sampling gives EML 63's net no reason to respect it and the paper never tests it.
- **Operator derivatives, verified.** CAD 193: `sensitiv` 0, `adjoint` 0, `compliance` 0, one `derivativ` hit (the strain operator). EML 63 writes no sensitivity equation despite needing one for OC and for §4.2's adjoint field. No derivative check exists in either paper. CAD 193 does set the presentation bar for a parameter sweep: 50 equidistant positions t ∈ [0,5] (§5.3) and five chamfers down to volume fraction 1/512 (§5.4), reporting κ and displacement error against the parameter.
- **A compliance-and-sensitivity contract.** CAD 193's displacement L2 errors are 2.9e-3 to 3.1e-2 (Table 2) and 6.4e-3 to 1.2e-2 (§5.3); it never reports a compliance or energy error. EML 63's single number is 7.6% on one scalar with the modelling error removed. Our 3% on assembled compliance *and* sensitivities is a strictly stronger claim on metrics neither paper reports — though 3% sits at the loose end of CAD 193's displacement band.
- **Exact cut discretisation.** CAD 193's Theorem 2 holds only "Suppose that the substructure problem in Eq. (6) for each coarse element D_α is well-conditioned", secured by voxelising the cut, averaging corner densities, and optionally adding soft material. Their measured κ improvement is 4–7 orders depending on the experiment (Table 2: 1.7e12 → 7.5e6 / 1.6e7 and 3.9e12 → 1.7e7 / 3.2e7, about 5 orders; §5.3 6–7; §5.4 about 4) — the abstract's "around 6 orders of magnitude" is the best of three. An exact CutFEM discretisation cannot make their hypothesis, and that is the sharpest technical wedge available to us.
- **Loadable immersed interfaces.** EML 63's Eq. (6) assumes zero interior load "without loss of generality". For a cut cell whose interior carries a loadable surface, that assumption fails — tractions live on nodes the condensation calls interior. Their own CutFEM future-work sentence is blocked by their own formulation.
- **A theoretical argument for the full trace, from their own appendix.** CAD 193 Lemma 4: "H h^{d-1} ||q_b||_2^2 ≲ ||Q_α||_{K_α}^2 ≲ h^{d-2} ||q_b||_2^2", and Eq. (25): "κ_2(K) = O(1/(H h ε^2)), for ε = min_{1≤I≤n_H}(||Ψ_I||_F)". Setting Ψ = I collapses ε and gives κ = O(1/(H h)), dimension-independent and ε-free. Their machinery says the un-parameterised trace has no boundary-interpolator degeneracy at all. Two lines from their Appendix A.
- **Locality.** Their reduced element is not per-cell: the keep/drop test runs on the *assembled* Ψ_I. A per-cell surrogate of their method inherits a non-local decision; our full trace has no such decision to make.
- **The error mode the full trace removes, in their words.** §3.3: "They bring in the issue of substructure boundaries being stiffer than it actually deforms in fine scale, also the so-called inter-element stiffness issue [11]. The Bézier coarse elements in Definition 5 resolve the issue by allowing for greater deformation flexibilities [15]." The linear → Bézier → C¹ B-spline progression in this literature is a march toward a richer trace; the un-parameterised nodal trace is its limit. EML 63 confirms the direction of travel and its ceiling — Conclusions (1) admits linear interpolation "will overestimate the stiffness of the substructure" and proposes "higher-order displacement interpolation schemes as in [41] with the cost of slightly increasing the number of unknowns." Every escape route in both papers is a richer *parameterisation*. Neither goes to the full nodal trace.

## 4. The gap between their proposal and our execution

**EML 63, Conclusions (3):** "The proposed approach may also be integrated seamlessly with CutFEM by learning the stiffness matrix of a cut element through the corresponding cutting patterns." One sentence, no method, no experiment, no number. Distance from us, precisely: the named object is an **un-condensed element-level** stiffness matrix, not a condensed cell operator — no trace, no Schur complement, no condensation appears in the clause; the named input is "cutting patterns", which reads as a discrete enumeration, not a continuous field (though the *preceding* sentence in the same paragraph does propose continuous geometry as input, control-point coordinates for IGA, so we cannot claim geometric input as unanticipated). Absent from the clause: condensation, trace dimension, equivariance, definiteness, conditioning, derivatives, loadable cut interfaces, and any quantity whatsoever. What they proposed is one voxel-level object; what we do is a 20,000-column condensed operator with a factored PSD encoding, group covariance to 1e-14, and a gated derivative. The distance is real — but it is the distance between a sentence and an implementation, not between two ideas.

**CAD 193, Section 6, item 1:** they name the bottleneck (per-element shape-function cost × many elements), name the remedy (deep neural networks), and cite three works including PIML and their own 3D CNN. This is closer to us than EML 63's sentence and is more dangerous, because it is precise about what to learn and why. Distance from us, precisely: what they propose learning is **Ψ-compressed**, the 168-DOF N_α / p_α, not S_α at the full trace — the thing they would accelerate is the object their own Appendix A shows to be a compression of ours. They give no trace target, no structural guarantee for a predicted operator, no equivariance, no derivative, and no optimisation. But they give the cost bar (19–23 ms/element) and they already own the learner. They are not proposing our method; they are proposing the cheaper version of it, from a position where the expensive version is one student-year away.

Stated without flattery: **neither paper is far from our idea, and both are far from our object.** Our defensible novelty is the un-parameterised nodal trace at q ≈ 20,000, the factored PSD encoding M_q = B^T A^{-1/2} B, exact cube-group covariance on genuinely cut cells, and a verified derivative gate — four specific things, not "learning the condensed operator", which is prior art in both bibliographies.

## 5. What to do about it

Reframe every claim against PIML (EML 63) and against CAD 193's Section 6 proposal rather than against an imagined absence of learned operators: cite EML 63's Fig. 9 result that direct operator prediction "reduce[s] the time ... by over 60%" while producing "a poor performance", cite the 24-DOF and 168-DOF trace sizes, and position the un-parameterised trace as the limit of the linear → Bézier → C¹ progression their own §3.3 says exists to cure "substructure boundaries being stiffer than it actually deforms." Concede the 2 s per cell openly against Table 2's 19–23 ms and defend it only on the two grounds that survive — ~120× wider output plus reuse across the lattice and across every optimisation iteration — and concede the free exact gradient d(K-tilde)/dρ = N^T (dK/dρ) N explicitly, presenting the derivative gate as the price of direct prediction rather than as a new capability. Add two gates the papers prove we need and we do not have: an inter-cell trace-consistency check, because CAD 193 attributes its second-order rate to "the continuity of the numerical shape functions on the coarse element boundaries" and independently predicted cells do not inherit it, and a conditioning diagnostic to replace the one we forfeit by discarding Ψ, reported with `condest` against Theorem 2's κ = O(1/(H h ε²)) and against the ε-free O(1/(H h)) that their Lemma 4 hands us for the full trace.
