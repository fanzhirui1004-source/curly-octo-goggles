## Supplementary Note S10. Transfer to a second TPMS family

The trained operator of the main text is specific to the Schwarz-P family of Eq. (1). This note records a test of how the construction carries over to a second family, the gyroid sheet, first without any retraining and then after a short continuation of training with a small number of gyroid cells. It is not part of the evidence of Sections 5–6 and is reported to delimit the scope of the trained weights, not of the construction.

**Cells.** A gyroid cell replaces the level-set function of Eq. (1) by \(\phi_G(x)=\sin2\pi x_1\cos2\pi x_2+\sin2\pi x_2\cos2\pi x_3+\sin2\pi x_3\cos2\pi x_1\), with the same box, trilinear corner parameters, optional planar cut, background mesh (\(n=32\)), material, ghost penalty, retained space and correction sequence (8 / \(Q_1(17)\) / 8) as the P cells. Every gyroid cell is the twin of one P cell: it keeps the P cell's position, cut plane and cell type, and its corner parameters are mapped from the P values so that the sheet volume fraction of the uniform-thickness cell is preserved (for example 23.3% against 23.2% and 5.8% against 6.2% for two twins). The cell bodies are generated by a level-set-generic version of the geometry stage, which reproduces the frozen P bodies of the \(2\times2\times2\) block of Section 5.9 cell by cell; in lattice mode it keeps the largest face-connected set of active elements of the whole lattice and removes fragments smaller than a quarter of an element that carry no ghost-penalty support, so that shared faces of neighbouring cells carry identical retained coordinates. The gyroid cells were not used in the training, validation or weight selection of any predictor of Table 2.

**Predictors.** *Zero-shot*: NICE with the weights of Table 2, applied to the gyroid cells without any change. *Continued* (NICE-G): the NICE weights continued for 8,000 updates on 96 P training cells and 18 gyroid twins of P training cells (cells with at most 420,000 degrees of freedom), with the correction inside the training loop as in Section 4.5; weights were selected on the P validation set only, so the gyroid evaluation cells entered neither training nor selection.

**Single cells.** Sixteen gyroid twins of the P validation cells of Section 5.1 (the twins of validation cells 2000–2015) were evaluated with 64 validation directions per loading class, the directional energy excess of Section 3.1 averaged over the directions of each cell and then over the sixteen cells with equal weights (Table ST28). Zero-shot, the mean energy excess under consistent tractions is 4.5% (largest cell 25.6%), 60 to 230 times the value on the P twins, and the degradation is systematic: every cell and every loading class is worse by one to two orders of magnitude, the smooth classes (polynomial and multiscale displacements) least. The hardest gyroid cells are the twins of the hardest P cells. The correction is still applied and still cannot increase the error, but the learned initial field no longer supplies what the correction does not reach: the network part of the extension is specific to the family it was trained on. After the continuation on 18 gyroid cells, the means fall by a factor of about eight (consistent tractions 0.74%, largest cell 2.7%), to about ten times the P level, while the P validation means change little (all class means at most 0.09% against 0.015–0.08% for NICE, Table ST28).

**Lattices.** The gyroid twin of the \(2\times2\times2\) block of Section 5.9 (eight gyroid cells, four of them cut, all learned) was solved as in Section 5.9 under the three consistent face loads and three random loads, with the exact condensed matrices as reference (Table ST29). Zero-shot, the lattice compliance errors are 0.98–1.5% under the consistent loads and up to 2.1% under the random loads, and the largest eight-corner sensitivity error over the cells is 13.5%; the preconditioned conjugate gradients take 434 iterations against 414 for the exact operators. With NICE-G the compliance errors fall to 0.23% (0.35% under the random loads) and the sensitivity errors to 1.8% (3.6%), while on the P block NICE-G gives 0.019% (0.084%) and 0.25% (0.49%) against 0.014% (0.069%) and 0.14% (0.45%) for NICE.

**Reading.** The variational properties of Section 4.1 hold on the gyroid cells as on the P cells, since they do not depend on the weights, and the correction reduces the error of every starting field; what does not transfer is the accuracy of the learned initial field, which is a property of the family it was trained on. Eighteen cells of the new family and 8,000 updates recover one order of magnitude; reaching the P level would require gyroid data and training of the order of those of Section 5.1. The construction therefore transfers to a new family with new training data, as stated in Section 6.4; the trained weights do not.
