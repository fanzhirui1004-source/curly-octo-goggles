## 2. Response-letter paragraphs for rebuttals

**I-42 (matched-accuracy cost comparison against a coarser conventional reference).** We agree that the surrogate's error should be compared with the discretisation error of the reference, and we now do so in Section 6.2. In heavily cut cells the surrogate's energy error lies well below the discretisation error at n = 32. Its largest assembled sensitivity errors, however, are comparable to the change of the reference sensitivities under refinement. We did not add a cost comparison against a conventional route on a coarser mesh chosen to match the surrogate's accuracy. A coarser mesh defines a different discrete model and therefore a different design problem: its compliance and sensitivities are not those of the model being optimised, and design sensitivities must be consistent with the discrete compliance that the optimiser minimises. A comparison at equal continuum accuracy would also require retraining NICE on the coarser discretisation, because the operator is tied to its background resolution (Section 7.5). Both routes are therefore compared on the same discrete model, which is the model that defines the design problem.

**I-52 (retaining the cut band when cut surfaces are traction-free).** We agree that the cut-band coordinates could be eliminated when the cut surfaces carry no load. We retain them so that loads and supports on cut surfaces, which occur at specimen boundaries and fixtures, remain available without retraining (Table ST06). In the lattices of Section 6.9 these coordinates are 37–50% of the free retained coordinates. The cost of the lattice iteration, however, is dominated by the cell applications, and these act on complete cells regardless of how their retained coordinates are partitioned. The global retained dimension enters mainly the retained-block factorisation and the coarse space, which take about 1 s and 6 s of a 110 s iteration for eight cells. Treating the band as interior is possible, but it would change the network's input space and require retraining. Section 7.1 now states this.

**R7: fair re-implementation of PIML-type substructures (I-35).** We agree that Section 6.8 cannot characterise PIML-type substructures. We have removed that attribution and the Introduction's quantitative statement. The comparison is now presented as an ablation of our own retained representation, with exact interiors, and its conditions are stated: one cell per substructure, fine-scale face tractions, no oversampling, and accuracy rather than accuracy at equal cost. The complete results are in the supplement. We have not re-implemented PIML with partition refinement, Bézier enrichment and overlapping bases on cut TPMS cells. Such a study would be our implementation of another group's method, applied outside its intended voxel-density setting, and without the original code and training data it could not be made fair to either side. The paper makes no claim about the accuracy of PIML. Its claim is narrower: for the cut cells and responses studied, removing boundary reduction places the entire approximation error in the interior, where the correction budget controls it. The ablation also reports the case that favours a reduced interface: restricting only the shared interface is adequate for compliance at low degree, but not for local sensitivity under neighbour loads.

**I-38 (Chebyshev interval verified with a different power-iteration start than the one used in the runs).** The premise is not correct, but it was caused by an inaccurate sentence in our Section 6.2, which we have corrected. The power iteration that estimates the Chebyshev endpoint uses a seeded generator on the device. The 80-geometry verification in Appendix D calls the same routine on the GPU, and its endpoints are identical to those of the GPU pair and lattice runs (for example M1: 5.198581 in both records). The operational endpoint used in every GPU result is therefore the one verified on all 80 validation geometries, with margins of 2.1–5.0%. The five per-cell margins in Table ST19 are different for a different reason: four of them come from a host evaluation whose generator produces a different start vector. The old sentence stated the opposite. We also note that symmetry, positive semidefiniteness and Ŝ ⪰ S hold for any endpoint, because Ŝ = FᵀKF with J_P F = I. Containment of the spectrum affects only the ordering in Eq. (17) and the accuracy.
