## 中文

### 1. 引言

梯度点阵 [Yu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2019.108021) 和按零件外形切割的点阵结构由大量几何各异的胞元组成。对这类结构进行厚度设计时存在两方面要求：结构规模大，每次设计迭代都需重新分析；而决定设计的量往往是局部的，例如靠近支撑的切割胞元的刚度，以及柔度对壁厚的灵敏度。均匀化方法以等效材料代替胞元，计算代价低，是梯度点阵设计的常用基础 [Li et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2018.06.003), [Wu et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-02881-8)；但在胞元尺寸不远小于结构尺寸处，尤其是在支撑处、载荷处和被零件边界切割的胞元中，其精度有限。对于第 5.10 节在切割处固支的单层板（图 1），均匀化模型将均匀初始设计的柔度低估了 27%，而本文方法仅低估 0.011%。对全部胞元壁进行细尺度分析可避免这一误差，借助大规模并行求解器亦可实现 [Aage et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23911)；但每次设计迭代都会改变所有胞元，细尺度分析须在每次迭代中重复进行。

子结构法介于上述两类方法之间。该方法将每个胞元视为超单元，通过静力凝聚将其缩减到与相邻胞元、支撑和载荷相连的主自由度上 [Guyan (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.2874), [Irons (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3027)。装配后的整体系统只含主自由度，各胞元的内部位移在求解后恢复，用于计算柔度和灵敏度。静力凝聚是精确的，但每种胞元几何都需要对内部刚度矩阵进行一次分解。对于切割薄壁胞元，主自由度包括胞元表面全部节点和切割平面单元全部节点的自由度；在本文的 80 个验证几何中，每个胞元的主自由度为 2,679 至 45,900 个，中位数约为 \(2.4\times10^4\)。由于每次设计迭代都改变所有胞元的几何，这些分解须在每次迭代中重做。

学习组件模型以神经网络代替内部求解。有的模型直接将节点位移映射为节点力 [Capuano & Rimoli (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.10.046)；学习子结构方法 [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041), [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955) 则以神经网络预测形函数，按 \(N^TKN\) 计算子结构刚度矩阵，并像有限元一样装配。为控制网络输出的规模，这类方法通常以线性或 Bézier 插值缩减边界自由度，网络为每个保留的边界自由度输出一个形函数。端口约化方法 [Eftang & Patera (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4543), [Smetana & Patera (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1137/15m1009603), [Ballani et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2017.09.014) 同样缩减组件之间共享的自由度。这种缩减限制了相邻子结构之间可传递的位移模式，由此产生的误差无法通过改进内部近似消除。对于切割胞元，切割面上的支撑和载荷要求保留切割平面单元的全部自由度，主自由度数目大且随几何变化，每个主自由度对应一个网络输出的做法难以直接适用。

本文提出 NICE 方法（neural-initialised static condensation with equilibrium correction，带平衡校正的神经初始化静力凝聚）。该方法保留胞元表面和切割平面单元的全部自由度，仅对内部位移进行近似。内部位移由以胞元几何为输入的神经网络预测。该网络对几何是非线性的，对主自由度位移则严格线性；其参数作用于单元局部节点位置、特征通道和网格层级，而不对应具体的自由度，因此约 \(6\times10^5\) 个参数的同一网络适用于主自由度数目各不相同的全部胞元。网络预测之后，在主自由度位移固定的条件下施加一次由 Chebyshev 光滑和粗网格校正组成的两重网格循环 [Xu (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1034116)，以减小内部不平衡力；方法名称中的平衡校正即指这一步。凝聚刚度矩阵由修正后位移场的应变能计算，计算过程中不形成任何矩阵。

由最小势能原理，按这种方式计算的凝聚刚度矩阵对称半正定，零空间仅含刚体模态，在矩阵意义上不小于精确凝聚刚度矩阵，且误差为内部位移误差的二次量；精确静力凝聚是内部位移精确时的特例。上述性质对任何精确再现主自由度位移和刚体运动的内部位移近似均成立，因而不依赖于网络的训练精度。两重网格修正是固定的线性运算，修正后的凝聚刚度矩阵保持上述性质；在光滑区间与粗网格求解满足一定条件时，其误差不超过修正前的误差 [Golub & Varga (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386013), [Xu & Zikatanov (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-02-00398-3)。网络在训练中经过这一修正，二者相互补充：在相同修正下，图调和初始场留下的误差是 NICE 的 5.4 至 265 倍（第 5.5 节）。

内部位移误差对柔度和灵敏度的影响不同。在柔度中，各胞元的误差按其应变能占比加权；在厚度灵敏度中，误差则经由刚度矩阵对厚度的导数以一次项进入。因此，应变能占比较小的胞元可使柔度保持准确，而其灵敏度仍有明显误差。拓扑优化中的近似重分析和非精确求解已观察到类似现象 [Amir et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.2536), [Amir et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-009-0463-4), [Gogu (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4797)。第 4 节对任意近似内部位移推导了这一关系，NICE 对柔度和灵敏度分别进行训练与检验。

本文的主要工作如下：

- 保留切割胞元表面和切割平面单元的全部自由度，只近似内部位移，使切割面上的支撑和载荷无需重新训练即可施加（第 2、5.7 和 5.10 节）。
- 构造对主自由度位移严格线性、规模与主自由度数目无关的神经网络来预测内部位移；由其应变能计算的凝聚刚度矩阵对称半正定，在矩阵意义上不小于精确凝聚刚度矩阵，且误差为内部位移误差的二次量（第 3 节）。
- 在凝聚计算中加入固定的两重网格修正，无需重新训练即可降低已训练网络的误差；在给定条件下修正不增大误差，且在所有测试算例中，增加修正步数均使误差进一步降低（第 3 和 5.5 节）。
- 推导内部位移误差进入柔度和灵敏度的不同途径，据此对二者分别检验，并在厚度设计中与精确静力凝聚和均匀化方法进行比较（第 4 和 5 节）。

第 2 节介绍切割胞元的有限元模型、静力凝聚与设计问题。第 3 节介绍 NICE 方法，包括凝聚刚度矩阵的计算方式、预测内部位移的网络、两重网格修正和训练方法。第 4 节分析内部位移误差对凝聚刚度矩阵、柔度和灵敏度的影响，相关证明见附录。第 5 节给出数值算例，包括单个胞元的精度、多胞元装配、计算成本和厚度优化。第 6 节讨论方法的适用范围与局限，第 7 节为结论。

## English

### 1. Introduction

Graded lattices [Yu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2019.108021) and lattices trimmed to the shape of a part consist of many cells of different geometry. Their thickness design faces two competing requirements. The structure is large and must be reanalysed at every design iteration, yet the quantities that drive the design are local, for example the stiffness of a cut cell next to a support or the sensitivity of the compliance to a wall thickness. Homogenisation replaces the cells by an equivalent material; it is inexpensive and is the usual basis for the design of graded lattices [Li et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2018.06.003), [Wu et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-02881-8). Its accuracy is limited, however, where the cells are not small compared with the structure, in particular at supports, at loads and in cells cut by the part boundary. For the plate of Section 5.10, a single layer clamped at its cut (Figure 1), the homogenised model underestimates the compliance of the uniform starting design by 27%, whereas the method of this paper underestimates it by 0.011%. A fine-scale analysis of every wall avoids this error and is feasible with large-scale parallel solvers [Aage et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23911), but each design iteration changes every cell, so the fine-scale analysis must be repeated at every iteration.

Substructuring lies between these two approaches. Each cell is treated as a superelement and statically condensed onto the retained (master) degrees of freedom through which it connects to its neighbours, supports and loads [Guyan (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.2874), [Irons (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3027). The assembled system contains only the retained degrees of freedom, and the interior displacements of each cell are recovered after the solve to evaluate the compliance and the sensitivities. Static condensation is exact, but it requires a factorisation of the interior stiffness matrix for every cell geometry. For cut thin-walled cells, the retained degrees of freedom are those of all nodes on the cell faces and of all nodes of the elements cut by the trimming plane; for the 80 validation geometries of this paper they number 2,679 to 45,900 per cell, with a median of about \(2.4\times10^4\). Because every design iteration changes the geometry of every cell, these factorisations must be repeated at every iteration.

Learned component models replace the interior solution by a neural network. Some map nodal displacements directly to nodal forces [Capuano & Rimoli (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.10.046); learned substructures [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041), [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955) predict shape functions with a neural network, compute the substructure stiffness matrix as \(N^TKN\) and assemble the substructures like finite elements. To limit the size of the network output, these methods reduce the boundary degrees of freedom by linear or Bézier interpolation, and the network outputs one shape function for each remaining boundary degree of freedom. Port reduction [Eftang & Patera (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4543), [Smetana & Patera (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1137/15m1009603), [Ballani et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2017.09.014) likewise reduces the degrees of freedom shared between components. Such a reduction restricts the displacement patterns that neighbouring substructures can exchange, and the resulting error cannot be removed by a better interior approximation. For cut cells, supports and loads on the cut surface require all degrees of freedom of the cut-plane elements to be kept, so the retained set is large and changes with the geometry, and a network with one output per retained degree of freedom does not apply directly.

This paper proposes NICE (neural-initialised static condensation with equilibrium correction). NICE keeps all degrees of freedom on the cell faces and in the cut-plane elements and approximates only the interior displacements. A neural network that takes the cell geometry as input predicts these displacements. The network is nonlinear in the geometry but exactly linear in the retained displacements, and its parameters act on local node positions, feature channels and grid levels rather than on individual degrees of freedom; one network of about \(6\times10^5\) parameters therefore serves all cells, whatever their number of retained degrees of freedom. After the prediction, one two-grid cycle of Chebyshev smoothing and coarse-grid correction [Xu (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1034116) is applied with the retained displacements held fixed, which reduces the interior force imbalance; this is the equilibrium correction in the name of the method. The condensed stiffness is computed from the strain energy of the corrected displacement field, and no matrix is formed.

By the principle of minimum potential energy, a condensed stiffness computed in this way is symmetric positive semidefinite, its null space contains only rigid-body modes, it is never softer than the exact condensed stiffness, and its error is quadratic in the interior displacement error; exact static condensation is the special case of exact interior displacements. These properties hold for any interior approximation that reproduces the retained displacements and rigid-body motions, and therefore do not depend on how well the network is trained. Because the two-grid correction is a fixed linear operation, the corrected condensed stiffness keeps these properties and, under conditions on the smoothing interval and the coarse solve, its error does not exceed that of the uncorrected one [Golub & Varga (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386013), [Xu & Zikatanov (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-02-00398-3). The network is trained through the correction, and the two complement each other: under the same correction, a graph-harmonic initial field leaves 5.4 to 265 times the error of NICE (Section 5.5).

An interior displacement error affects the compliance and the sensitivities differently. In the compliance, the error of each cell is weighted by the fraction of the strain energy that the cell carries; in the thickness sensitivity, it enters through the derivative of the stiffness matrix as a first-order term. A cell that carries a small fraction of the energy can therefore leave the compliance accurate while its sensitivity remains inaccurate. Similar observations have been made for approximate reanalysis and inexact solves in topology optimisation [Amir et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.2536), [Amir et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-009-0463-4), [Gogu (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4797). Section 4 derives this relation for any approximate interior displacement, and NICE is trained and checked on compliance and sensitivity separately.

The main contributions are as follows.

- All degrees of freedom on the faces of a cut cell and in its cut-plane elements are retained, and only the interior displacements are approximated, so that supports and loads on the cut surface can be applied without retraining (Sections 2, 5.7 and 5.10).
- A neural network that is exactly linear in the retained displacements, and whose size does not depend on their number, predicts the interior displacements; the condensed stiffness computed from its strain energy is symmetric positive semidefinite, never softer than the exact one, and has an error quadratic in the interior displacement error (Section 3).
- A fixed two-grid correction inside the condensation reduces the error of a trained network without retraining; under stated conditions it does not increase the error, and in all cases tested a larger number of smoothing steps reduced the error further (Sections 3 and 5.5).
- The different routes by which the interior error reaches the compliance and the sensitivities are derived, both are checked separately, and thickness designs are compared with exact static condensation and with homogenisation (Sections 4 and 5).

Section 2 describes the finite element model of the cut cells, static condensation and the design problem. Section 3 presents NICE: the computation of the condensed stiffness, the network that predicts the interior displacements, the two-grid correction and the training. Section 4 analyses how the interior displacement error affects the condensed stiffness, the compliance and the sensitivities; the proofs are given in the appendices. Section 5 presents numerical examples: the accuracy on single cells, assemblies of several cells, the computational cost and thickness optimisation. Section 6 discusses the scope and limitations of the method, and Section 7 concludes.
