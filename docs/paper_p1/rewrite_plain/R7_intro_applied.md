# 中文

## 1. 引言

点阵结构可通过胞元厚度的梯度分布调节各部位的刚度 [Yu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2019.108021)。梯度点阵的厚度设计以各胞元的厚度参数为设计变量，通过迭代优化确定其分布。厚度设计依赖于局部响应，如支撑和载荷附近胞元的刚度以及柔度对各胞元厚度的灵敏度，因此须准确计算各胞元的响应；同时，每次设计迭代均需重新分析整个结构，单次分析的计算量随模型规模增大，并在优化过程中不断累积。分析精度要求模型分辨胞元尺度的变形，计算效率则要求模型规模尽可能小，二者相互制约。

细尺度有限元模型直接离散全部胞元，能够满足精度要求；借助大规模并行求解器，此类模型已用于极大规模的拓扑优化 [Aage et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23911)。然而，每次设计迭代都会改变全部胞元的几何，细尺度模型须在每次迭代中重新组装和求解（第 5.9 节）。均匀化方法以等效材料代替胞元，计算代价低，是梯度点阵设计的常用方法 [Li et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2018.06.003), [Wu et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-02881-8)。该方法对单胞采用周期性边界条件假设，这一假设在不满足尺度分离或胞元被切割时不再成立：在支撑、载荷和边界附近，位移场的变化尺度与胞元尺寸相当；点阵按零件外形切割后，边界处的切割胞元也不再是完整的周期单胞。对于第 5.10 节在切割处固支的单层板（图 1），均匀化模型将均匀初始设计的柔度低估了 27%。

**图 1. 按零件外形切割的点阵，其切割胞元承担支撑。** (a) 单层 \(8\times4\) 个 Schwarz-P 胞元（式 (1)，均匀 \(\tau=0.40\)）经一个平面切割后的俯视图。该层含 16 个未切割胞元（浅色）和 8 个切割胞元（深色）；被切去的部分以浅色虚线框绘出。板在切割处固支：每个切割胞元中切割平面单元的全部自由度均被固定（阴影线）。端面承受合力为单位值的面内面力载荷（箭头）。(b) (a) 中框出的切割胞元，壁面半透明，并绘出切割平面。其 \(Q_2\) 模型共有 72,631 个节点，其中位于胞元表面（蓝色）和切割平面单元中（沙色）的 9,674 个节点被保留。其余节点均为内部节点，经静力凝聚消去。曲面由式 (1) 重构，仅用于显示。

子结构法介于上述两类方法之间。该方法将每个胞元视为一个子结构（超单元），通过静力凝聚消去其内部自由度，只保留与相邻胞元、支撑和载荷相连的主自由度 [Guyan (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.2874), [Irons (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3027)。整体方程组仅在主自由度上组装和求解，各胞元的内部位移在求解后恢复，用于计算灵敏度等局部量。对于切割薄壁胞元，主自由度包括胞元表面全部节点以及与切割平面相交的单元（以下称切割平面单元）全部节点上的自由度；在本文的 80 个验证几何中，单个胞元的主自由度为 2,679 至 45,900 个，中位数约为 \(2.4\times10^4\)，内部自由度的数目则为其数倍（图 1b）。静力凝聚不引入近似，但须对每个胞元的内部刚度矩阵进行稀疏 Cholesky 分解，并据此形成稠密的凝聚刚度矩阵。主自由度数目为中位数的胞元，其凝聚刚度矩阵在双精度下占用 4.2 GiB；对于第 5.9 节的部署胞元，单个胞元的 Cholesky 因子占用 0.60–9.4 GiB。每次设计迭代都会改变全部胞元的几何，这些分解须对全部胞元重新进行；在第 5.9 节的点阵上，常规精确静力凝聚一次分析的耗时均超过整体点阵的直接求解。

学习组件模型以神经网络给出单元或子结构的刚度关系，分析中无需求解组件内部的平衡问题。例如，[Capuano & Rimoli (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.10.046) 以神经网络直接建立单元节点位移到节点力的映射；学习子结构方法 [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041), [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955) 以神经网络预测数值形函数，按 \(N^TKN\) 计算子结构刚度矩阵，并按有限元的方式组装。为控制网络的输出规模，学习子结构方法以少量自由度描述子结构的边界位移，例如采用线性插值或三次 Bézier 插值，并为每个边界自由度输出一个形函数；端口约化方法 [Eftang & Patera (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4543), [Smetana & Patera (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1137/15m1009603), [Ballani et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2017.09.014) 同样缩减组件之间共享的自由度。边界自由度的缩减限定了相邻子结构之间可传递的位移分布，由此引入的模型误差不能通过提高内部近似的精度来消除。对于切割胞元，切割面上的支撑和载荷施加于切割平面单元的全部节点，这些节点上的自由度须全部作为主自由度保留，因而不宜以少量自由度描述其边界位移；同时，主自由度数目较大且随胞元几何变化，也难以为每个主自由度设置一个网络输出。

为此，本文提出带平衡校正的神经初始化静力凝聚方法（neural-initialised static condensation with equilibrium correction，NICE）。该方法保留胞元表面和切割平面单元上的全部自由度，仅对内部位移进行近似：以胞元几何为输入的神经网络给出内部位移的初值，再在主自由度位移固定的条件下施加一次由 Chebyshev 光滑和粗网格校正组成的两重网格循环 [Xu (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1034116)，以减小内部不平衡力。网络对几何为非线性，对主自由度位移严格线性；其参数作用于单元局部节点位置、特征通道和网格层级，而不与具体自由度对应，因此约 \(6\times10^5\) 个参数的同一网络可用于主自由度数目不同的胞元。光滑主要减小内部误差的高频分量，粗网格校正则减小其低频分量。网络与修正共同确定由主自由度位移到胞元全部位移的线性映射，凝聚刚度矩阵由所得位移场的应变能计算。装配方程组以预条件共轭梯度法求解，只需计算凝聚刚度矩阵与向量的乘积；该乘积由网络、修正、胞元刚度矩阵及修正和网络的转置依次作用得到，因此无需形成该映射和凝聚刚度矩阵。

若内部位移的近似对主自由度位移呈线性，并精确再现主自由度位移和刚体运动，则由其应变能计算的凝聚刚度矩阵对称半正定，零能模态仅为刚体运动；由最小势能原理，该矩阵构成精确凝聚刚度矩阵的上界，其误差为内部位移误差的二阶小量，内部位移精确时二者相同。NICE 的网络由构造满足上述条件，因此这些性质不依赖于网络的训练精度。两重网格修正为固定的线性运算，修正后上述条件仍然满足；当光滑区间和粗网格求解满足命题 2 的条件时，修正不会增大该误差 [Golub & Varga (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386013), [Xu & Zikatanov (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-02-00398-3)。网络在训练中即包含这一修正，二者作用互补：在相同修正下，不含可训练参数的图调和初始场所遗留的平均能量误差为 NICE 的 5.4 至 265 倍（第 5.5 节）。

内部位移误差对柔度和灵敏度的影响并不相同。柔度误差是各胞元误差按其应变能占比的加权和，为内部位移误差的二阶小量；厚度灵敏度则经由刚度矩阵对厚度的导数，含有内部位移误差的一阶项，能量误差对该项只能给出平方根量级的界。因此，应变能占比小的胞元几乎不影响柔度，但其灵敏度仍可能不准确。这一现象并不限于静力凝聚：凡以近似位移场计算柔度和灵敏度，柔度的精度都不能保证灵敏度的精度；在拓扑优化的近似重分析和非精确求解中，已有研究观察到类似现象 [Amir et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.2536), [Amir et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-009-0463-4), [Gogu (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4797)。第 4 节对满足上述条件的任意内部位移近似推导了这些关系（命题 3 和命题 4），据此，NICE 在训练与检验中同时考察柔度和灵敏度（第 5.6 节）。

本文的主要工作如下：

- 保留切割胞元表面和切割平面单元上的全部自由度，仅近似内部位移；尽管这些自由度随几何变化，切割面上的支撑和载荷仍作用于原有自由度，无需重新训练即可施加（第 2、5.7 和 5.10 节）。
- 构造预测内部位移的神经网络，其输出由构造保证对主自由度位移严格线性，并精确再现主自由度位移和刚体运动；网络规模与主自由度数目无关，计算中不形成矩阵。由其应变能计算的凝聚刚度矩阵对称半正定，构成精确凝聚刚度矩阵的上界，误差为内部位移误差的二阶小量（第 3.1、3.2 和 3.6 节）。
- 在凝聚计算中引入固定的两重网格修正，无需重新训练即可降低已训练网络的误差。对任何刚度矩阵对称半正定、内部块正定的离散模型，修正均保持上述性质，并在命题 2 的条件下不增大能量误差（第 3.3 和 3.4 节）；在补充表 ST07 的全部算例中，增加光滑步数均使能量误差进一步降低（第 5.5 节）。
- 推导内部位移误差进入柔度和灵敏度的不同途径，据此对二者分别检验，并将厚度设计与精确静力凝聚核对、与均匀化方法比较（第 4、5.6、5.8 和 5.10 节）。

第 1.1 节综述相关工作。第 2 节介绍切割胞元的有限元模型、静力凝聚、设计问题和基本假设。第 3 节介绍 NICE 方法，包括凝聚刚度矩阵的计算、预测内部位移的网络、两重网格修正、训练方法，以及不显式形成凝聚刚度矩阵的施加方式。第 4 节分析内部位移误差对柔度和灵敏度的影响，相关证明见附录。第 5 节给出数值算例，研究对象为稳定化切割有限元模型中的切割薄壁 Schwarz-P 胞元，内容包括：80 个验证几何和两胞元装配上的精度，限制胞元表面位移的消融，由训练和检查点选择均未使用的胞元构成的八胞元点阵，与整体点阵直接求解及常规精确静力凝聚的计算成本比较，以及与精确静力凝聚核对、并与均匀化模型比较的厚度优化。第 6 节讨论方法的适用范围与局限，第 7 节为结论。

# English

## 1. Introduction

The stiffness of a lattice can be tailored by grading the thickness of its cells [Yu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.matdes.2019.108021). In the thickness design of a graded lattice, the thickness parameters of the cells are the design variables, and their distribution is determined by iterative optimisation. The design depends on local responses, such as the stiffness of the cells next to supports and loads and the sensitivity of the compliance to the thickness of each cell, so the response of every cell must be computed accurately. At the same time, the whole structure is reanalysed at every design iteration, and the cost of each analysis, which grows with the size of the model, accumulates over the optimisation. Accuracy thus calls for a model that resolves the deformation at the scale of the cells, whereas efficiency calls for a model that is as small as possible.

Fine-scale finite element models discretise every cell and meet the accuracy requirement; with large-scale parallel solvers, such models have been used in topology optimisation at very large scale [Aage et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23911). Because every design iteration changes the geometry of every cell, however, a fine-scale model must be reassembled and solved again at every iteration (Section 5.9). Homogenisation replaces the cells by an equivalent material; it is inexpensive and is the usual basis for the design of graded lattices [Li et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2018.06.003), [Wu et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-021-02881-8). It assumes periodic boundary conditions on a unit cell, an assumption that fails without scale separation or when cells are cut: near supports, loads and boundaries, the displacement field varies on the scale of a cell, and when a lattice is trimmed to the shape of a part, the cut cells at its boundary are no longer complete periodic cells. For the plate of Section 5.10, a single layer of cells clamped at its cut (Figure 1), the homogenised model underestimates the compliance of the uniform starting design by 27%.

Substructuring lies between these two approaches. Each cell is treated as a substructure, or superelement, whose interior degrees of freedom are eliminated by static condensation, so that only the retained (master) degrees of freedom through which the cell connects to its neighbours, supports and loads remain [Guyan (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.2874), [Irons (1965)](https://doi.org/10.2514/3.3027). The global system is assembled and solved on the retained degrees of freedom only, and the interior displacements of each cell are recovered afterwards to evaluate local quantities such as the sensitivities. For cut thin-walled cells, the retained degrees of freedom are those of all nodes on the cell faces and of all nodes of the elements intersected by the trimming plane, hereafter referred to as cut-plane elements; for the 80 validation geometries of this paper, a cell has 2,679 to 45,900 retained degrees of freedom, with a median of about \(2.4\times10^4\), and several times as many interior ones (Figure 1b). Static condensation introduces no approximation, but it requires a sparse Cholesky factorisation of the interior stiffness matrix of every cell, from which a dense condensed stiffness matrix is formed. For a cell with the median number of retained degrees of freedom, this matrix occupies 4.2 GiB in double precision, and for the deployed cells of Section 5.9 the Cholesky factor of a single cell occupies 0.60–9.4 GiB. Because every design iteration changes the geometry of every cell, these factorisations must be repeated for all cells at every iteration; on the lattices of Section 5.9, one analysis by conventional exact static condensation takes longer than a direct solve of the whole lattice.

Learned component models obtain the stiffness relation of an element or a substructure from a neural network, so that no interior equilibrium problem of the component has to be solved during the analysis. For example, [Capuano & Rimoli (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.10.046) map the nodal displacements of an element directly to its nodal forces, and learned substructures [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041), [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955) predict numerical shape functions with a neural network, compute the substructure stiffness matrix as \(N^TKN\) and assemble the substructures in the manner of finite elements. To keep the network output small, learned substructures describe the boundary displacement of each substructure by a few degrees of freedom, for example through linear or cubic Bézier interpolation, and the network outputs one shape function for each boundary degree of freedom; port reduction [Eftang & Patera (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4543), [Smetana & Patera (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1137/15m1009603), [Ballani et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2017.09.014) likewise reduces the degrees of freedom shared between components. Reducing the boundary degrees of freedom restricts the displacements that neighbouring substructures can exchange, and the resulting model error cannot be removed by a more accurate interior approximation. For cut cells, the supports and loads on the cut surface act on all nodes of the cut-plane elements, whose degrees of freedom must therefore all be retained, so a description of the boundary displacement by a few degrees of freedom is unsuitable. Since the retained degrees of freedom are numerous and vary with the cell geometry, one network output per retained degree of freedom is also impractical.

This paper therefore proposes neural-initialised static condensation with equilibrium correction (NICE). NICE retains all degrees of freedom on the cell faces and in the cut-plane elements and approximates only the interior displacements: a neural network that takes the cell geometry as input provides an initial interior displacement field, and one two-grid cycle of Chebyshev smoothing and coarse-grid correction [Xu (1992)](https://doi.org/10.1137/1034116), applied with the retained displacements held fixed, reduces the interior force imbalance. The network is nonlinear in the geometry but exactly linear in the retained displacements. Its parameters act on local node positions, feature channels and grid levels rather than on individual degrees of freedom, so that one network of about \(6\times10^5\) parameters serves cells with different numbers of retained degrees of freedom. The smoothing mainly reduces the high-frequency part of the interior error, and the coarse-grid correction reduces its smooth part. The network and the correction together define a linear map from the retained displacements to the displacements of the whole cell, and the condensed stiffness is computed from the strain energy of the resulting displacement field. The assembled system is solved by preconditioned conjugate gradients, which need only products of the condensed stiffness with vectors; each product is obtained by applying the network, the correction, the cell stiffness matrix and the transposes of the correction and the network in turn, so neither the map nor the condensed stiffness has to be formed.

If the interior displacement approximation is linear in the retained displacements and reproduces them and rigid-body motions, the condensed stiffness computed from its strain energy is symmetric positive semidefinite, with rigid-body motions as its only zero-energy modes. By the principle of minimum potential energy, it is then an upper bound on the exact condensed stiffness, with an error of second order in the interior displacement error, and the two coincide when the interior displacements are exact. The network of NICE satisfies these conditions by construction, so these properties do not depend on its training accuracy. Since the two-grid correction is a fixed linear operation, the conditions still hold after the correction; when the smoothing interval and the coarse solve satisfy the conditions of Proposition 2, the correction does not increase the error [Golub & Varga (1961)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01386013), [Xu & Zikatanov (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0894-0347-02-00398-3). The network is trained with the correction applied, and the two complement each other: under the same correction, a graph-harmonic initial field, which has no trainable parameters, leaves 5.4 to 265 times the mean energy error of NICE (Section 5.5).

The interior displacement error affects the compliance and the sensitivities differently. The compliance error is a sum of cell errors weighted by the fractions of the strain energy that the cells carry, and is of second order in the interior displacement error. The thickness sensitivities, by contrast, contain a term of first order in this error, introduced through the derivative of the stiffness matrix with respect to the thickness, which the energy error bounds only at the order of its square root. A cell that carries a small fraction of the energy therefore has little effect on the compliance but may still have inaccurate sensitivities. This is not specific to static condensation: whenever the compliance and the sensitivities are computed from an approximate displacement field, an accurate compliance does not ensure accurate sensitivities, and similar observations have been made for approximate reanalysis and inexact solves in topology optimisation [Amir et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.2536), [Amir et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00158-009-0463-4), [Gogu (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.4797). Section 4 derives these relations for any interior approximation that meets the conditions above (Propositions 3 and 4); accordingly, NICE is trained and checked on both the compliance and the sensitivities (Section 5.6).

The main contributions are as follows.

- All degrees of freedom on the faces of a cut cell and in its cut-plane elements are retained, and only the interior displacements are approximated. Although these degrees of freedom change with the geometry, supports and loads on the cut surface act on the original degrees of freedom and can be applied without retraining (Sections 2, 5.7 and 5.10).
- A neural network for the interior displacements is constructed whose output is, by construction, exactly linear in the retained displacements and reproduces them and rigid-body motions; its size does not depend on the number of retained degrees of freedom, and it forms no matrix. The condensed stiffness computed from its strain energy is symmetric positive semidefinite and an upper bound on the exact condensed stiffness, with an error of second order in the interior displacement error (Sections 3.1, 3.2 and 3.6).
- A fixed two-grid correction is introduced into the condensation and reduces the error of a trained network without retraining. It preserves these properties for any discrete model with a symmetric positive semidefinite stiffness matrix and a positive definite interior block, and under the conditions of Proposition 2 it does not increase the energy error (Sections 3.3 and 3.4); in every example of Supplementary Table ST07, a larger number of smoothing steps reduced the energy error further (Section 5.5).
- The different routes by which the interior displacement error enters the compliance and the sensitivities are derived. Both quantities are checked accordingly, and the thickness designs are verified against exact static condensation and compared with homogenisation (Sections 4, 5.6, 5.8 and 5.10).

Section 1.1 reviews related work. Section 2 describes the finite element model of the cut cells, static condensation, the design problem and the assumptions of the analysis. Section 3 presents NICE: the computation of the condensed stiffness, the network that predicts the interior displacements, the two-grid correction, the training and the application of the condensed stiffness without forming it. Section 4 analyses the effect of the interior displacement error on the compliance and the sensitivities; the proofs are given in the appendices. Section 5 presents numerical examples for cut thin-walled Schwarz-P cells in a stabilised cut finite element model: the accuracy on 80 validation geometries and on two-cell assemblies, an ablation that restricts the cell-face displacements, eight-cell lattices of cells used neither in training nor in checkpoint selection, the computational cost compared with a direct solve of the whole lattice and with conventional exact static condensation, and thickness optimisation checked against exact static condensation and compared with a homogenised model. Section 6 discusses the scope and limitations of the method, and Section 7 concludes.
