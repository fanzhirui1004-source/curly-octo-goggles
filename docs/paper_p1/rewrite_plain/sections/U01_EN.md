### 1.1. Related work

Among learned substructures, problem-independent machine learning (PIML) has been studied most systematically. PIML predicts numerical shape functions of a substructure for a few boundary degrees of freedom and imposes constraints for rigid-body motion; its self-consistent form computes the substructure stiffness matrix as \(N^TKN\) [Huang et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2022.101887), [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041). Its variants train without precomputed shape-function data by minimising the potential energy [Huang et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2024.105893), impose symmetry equivariance [Jiang, C. et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2026.120865), treat isoparametric substructures [Zhang, L. et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2024.102237), transfer learned substructures to complex three-dimensional design domains [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmecsci.2026.112007) and optimise lattices [Xu et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2025.119330). One variant joins oversampled numerical basis functions by an overlapping partition of unity and corrects the reconstructed fine-scale field by a few Jacobi-preconditioned conjugate gradient iterations on the global equilibrium equations [Guo et al. (2026b)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22019v1). A few boundary degrees of freedom per substructure keep the assembled model small enough for very large structures, and the resulting boundary model error is controlled by refining the partition, enriching the interpolation or oversampling. NICE lies at the other end of this trade-off: it keeps all retained degrees of freedom of a cell, which avoids this boundary model error but enlarges the assembled model. It learns the interior displacements of each geometry in a family whose discrete space changes with the cut, instead of numerical shape functions for a fixed set of boundary degrees of freedom. Another PIML variant replaces the linear interpolation of the boundary displacement by cubic Bézier interpolation and was compared, in the same study, with a variant that predicts numerical shape functions for every boundary node. For voxel substructures, the latter cost more than it saved and was less accurate on the larger substructures, because its network had to predict many more outputs [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955). In NICE, condensation still removes most unknowns, because the interior of a thin-walled cell is several times larger than its retained part; the cell of Figure 1b retains 9,674 of its 72,631 nodes. The network size of NICE does not grow with the number of retained degrees of freedom, and the two-grid correction reduces the error that the network leaves (Section 5.5).

Closest to NICE are component models that keep a mechanical structure. A condensed stiffness can be computed from the strain energy of an approximate displacement field that is linear in the retained displacements and reproduces them and rigid-body motions. By the principle of minimum potential energy, this condensed stiffness is never softer than the exact one, and its error is quadratic in the interior displacement error (Section 3.1). The same property underlies static condensation itself [Toselli & Widlund (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1007/b137868), multiscale finite elements [Hou & Wu (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1997.5682), component reduced-basis methods [Huynh et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2012022) and learned shape functions evaluated as \(N^TKN\) [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041). NICE applies it to network-predicted interior displacements on a set of retained degrees of freedom that changes with the cut. Learned and reduced component models differ in three respects: the exchange between a component and its neighbours, the representation of the interior and the place where the approximation is improved. The learned substructures of [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041) and [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955) reduce what a component exchanges and learn shape functions for the reduced boundary. NICE keeps everything a component exchanges and learns only the response of the interior. It represents the interior by a network that takes the cell geometry as input and improves the approximation within the condensed stiffness that is assembled.

As to what a component exchanges, component reduced-basis methods keep the complete interface in the condensation but represent the interior on a discrete space shared by the parameter family and construct reduced bases for it offline [Huynh et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2012022). For lattices, such component-wise models have been reused in design optimisation, with error bounds on the displacement, the compliance and its sensitivity [McBane & Choi (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.113813). For unfitted discretisations, solutions on varying active sets have been extended to a common background space before reduction [Chasapi et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2023.115997). NICE also keeps the complete set of retained degrees of freedom, but this set itself changes with the cut, and its network therefore takes the discrete geometry of each cell as input, instead of the parameters of a shared discretisation.

The representation of a component varies among lattice methods and learned elements. One lattice method condenses the cells into superelements and interpolates their stiffness in a density parameter by a reduced-order surrogate, which supplies explicit stiffness derivatives for optimisation without scale separation [Wu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.11.003). For lattices built by spline composition, reduced-order cell operators have replaced the cell-wise local solves of inexact finite element tearing and interconnecting, dual–primal (FETI-DP) methods [Hirschler et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.7419). For unfitted two-dimensional lattices, balancing domain decomposition by constraints (BDDC) has been combined with full cell stiffness matrices, whose integrals over trimmed cells are approximated by matrix discrete empirical interpolation [Bonilla Moreno et al. (2027)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.119304). Each of these lattice methods keeps one discretisation across cells: a common lattice pattern, a composed parametric cell and one high-order unfitted element per cell, respectively. For unfitted coarse elements, numerical shape functions have also been conditioned against small cuts [Chen & Li (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2026.104038). Learned elements condense an embedded inner domain into a symmetric positive semidefinite interface stiffness [Parish et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00466-024-02481-5). Convex neural energy elements learn boundary energies from targets computed with the exact condensed stiffness and recover the interior fields by exact lifting [Jiang, H. et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02036v1). NICE learns the displacement recovery itself. A single corrected recovery operator \(F\), built without an interior factorisation and applied without forming a matrix, defines the assembled condensed stiffness, the recovered displacements and the thickness sensitivities.

The place where the approximation is improved also differs. Inexact substructuring methods [Dohrmann (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.514), [Li & Widlund (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2006.03.011), [Klawonn & Rheinbach (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1758) improve approximate subdomain and coarse solves inside a preconditioner, and their iteration converges to the exact discrete solution (Section 6.2). In a nonlinear example, convex neural energy elements refine the exact lifting by Newton steps and differentiate through these steps [Jiang, H. et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02036v1). Component reduced-basis methods bound the error of the component or assembled solution a posteriori [Huynh et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2012022). NICE provides a computable interior residual instead of a certified bound (Eq. (7)). Its correction acts on the interior displacements of each cell at fixed retained displacements, inside the strain energy that defines the condensed stiffness, and therefore changes the assembled condensed stiffness.

Learning has also been combined with multilevel and domain decomposition solvers. Examples are learned multigrid prolongations [Greenfeld et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/greenfeld19a.html), [Luz et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/luz20a.html), machine-learning-assisted adaptive FETI-DP coarse spaces [Heinlein et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1344913) and networks trained through the solver [Um et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/43e4e6a6f341e00671e123714de019a8-Abstract.html). Hybrid neural solvers combine learned predictions with relaxation, learned inverse actions or coarse spaces [Zhang, E. et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00910-x), [Kopaničáková & Karniadakis (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1137/24m162861x), [Xing et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3811333). In optimisation without scale separation, spectral multiscale coarse bases [Efendiev et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.04.045) have served as the coarse space of a two-level preconditioner for the fully resolved problem [Alexandersen & Lazarov (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2015.02.028). Geometry-aware neural operators and graph networks [Li et al. (2023a)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v24/23-0064.html), [Li et al. (2023b)](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-1556), [Pfaff et al. (2021)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=roNqYL0_XP), multigrid-structured operators [He et al. (2024)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/eb3c8135137c8a60425a0320869ad87e-Abstract-Conference.html), learned Green's functions [Boullé & Townsend (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-022-09556-w) and training by energy minimisation [E & Yu (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40304-018-0127-z) learn the solution map of the whole problem. Most neural operators learn maps that are nonlinear in their input and do not obey superposition. The strain energy of the predicted field is then not a quadratic form in the retained displacements, and the properties of the condensed stiffness rest on this quadratic form. The methods above accelerate a global solve or approximate the solution of the whole problem. In NICE, the learned map recovers the displacements of a single cell from its retained displacements, and the strain energy of these displacements defines a condensed stiffness that can be assembled, differentiated and improved.

Static condensation is itself a two-level method. The exact recovery \(E\) maps the retained displacements to the equilibrium displacements of the whole cell. In algebraic multigrid terms, \(E\) is the ideal interpolation from the retained to all degrees of freedom, and \(S=E^TKE\) is the corresponding coarse-grid matrix [Falgout & Vassilevski (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142903429742). NICE replaces \(E\) by a recovery operator \(F\) from the retained to the active degrees of freedom and condenses with the coarse-grid matrix \(\widehat S=F^TKF\) of this interpolation; a network predicts this operator, and the two-grid cycle then smooths it. Smoothed-aggregation multigrid similarly improves a tentative prolongation by smoothing [Vaněk et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02238511). The operator \(F\) is nonlinear in the cell geometry and linear in the retained displacements. Learned multigrid prolongations [Greenfeld et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/greenfeld19a.html), [Luz et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/luz20a.html) are likewise nonlinear in the operator and linear in the vector they act on, but they serve as part of a solver. The operator \(F\) instead defines a component model: it serves sets of retained degrees of freedom that change with the cut, and its coarse-grid matrix is assembled into the lattice and differentiated in design.
