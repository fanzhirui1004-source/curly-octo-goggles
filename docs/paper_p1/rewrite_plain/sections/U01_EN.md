### 1.1. Related work

Static condensation underlies substructuring and domain decomposition methods [Toselli & Widlund (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1007/b137868). Building on it, multiscale finite elements use solutions of local problems as the basis functions of coarse elements [Hou & Wu (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcph.1997.5682), and component reduced-basis methods keep the complete component interface in the condensation, construct reduced bases for the interior offline on a discrete space shared by the parameter family and bound the error of the component or assembled solution a posteriori [Huynh et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1051/m2an/2012022). In all of these methods, the condensed stiffness is computed from the strain energy of an approximate displacement field. When this field is linear in the interface displacements and reproduces them and rigid-body motions, the principle of minimum potential energy makes the condensed stiffness an upper bound on the exact one, with an error of second order in the interior displacement error (Section 3.1).

Condensation and reduction have been used in several ways for lattices. Cells have been condensed into superelements whose stiffness is interpolated in a density parameter by a reduced-order surrogate, which supplies explicit stiffness derivatives for optimisation without scale separation [Wu et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.11.003). Component-wise reduced models have been reused in lattice design optimisation, with error bounds on the displacement, the compliance and its sensitivity [McBane & Choi (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2021.113813), and for lattices built by spline composition, reduced-order cell operators have replaced the cell-wise local solves of inexact finite element tearing and interconnecting, dual–primal (FETI-DP) methods [Hirschler et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.7419). For unfitted discretisations, [Chasapi et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2023.115997) extended solutions on varying active sets to a common background space before reduction, [Chen & Li (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cad.2026.104038) improved the conditioning of numerical shape functions under small cuts, and [Bonilla Moreno et al. (2027)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.119304) combined balancing domain decomposition by constraints (BDDC) with full cell stiffness matrices for two-dimensional lattices, approximating the integrals over trimmed cells by matrix discrete empirical interpolation.

Learned component models obtain the stiffness relation of an element or a substructure from a neural network. [Capuano & Rimoli (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2018.10.046) map the nodal displacements of an element directly to its nodal forces. Problem-independent machine learning (PIML) predicts numerical shape functions for a few boundary degrees of freedom of a substructure, imposes constraints for rigid-body motion and computes the substructure stiffness matrix as \(N^TKN\) [Huang et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2022.101887), [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041). Later work extended the training, the treatment of symmetry, the substructure types, the boundary interpolation and the global correction [Huang et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmps.2024.105893), [Jiang, C. et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2026.120865), [Zhang, L. et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2024.102237), [Guo et al. (2026a)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2026.118955), [Guo et al. (2026b)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22019v1), and applied learned substructures to complex three-dimensional design domains and to lattice optimisation [Zhang et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmecsci.2026.112007), [Xu et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compstruct.2025.119330). These methods describe the displacements exchanged between substructures by a few boundary degrees of freedom, which keeps the assembled model small, and they control the resulting boundary error by refining the partition, raising the order of the boundary interpolation or oversampling. In addition, learned elements condense an embedded inner domain into a symmetric positive semidefinite interface stiffness [Parish et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00466-024-02481-5), and convex neural energy elements learn boundary energies from targets computed with the exact condensed stiffness, recover the interior fields by exact lifting and, in a nonlinear example, refine this lifting by Newton steps [Jiang, H. et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02036v1).

Among solvers, inexact substructuring methods use approximate subdomain and coarse solves inside the preconditioner, and their iteration still converges to the exact discrete solution [Dohrmann (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nla.514), [Li & Widlund (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2006.03.011), [Klawonn & Rheinbach (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.1758); in optimisation without scale separation, spectral multiscale coarse bases [Efendiev et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2013.04.045) have served as the coarse space of a two-level preconditioner for the fully resolved problem [Alexandersen & Lazarov (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cma.2015.02.028). Learning has been combined with such multilevel and domain decomposition solvers, for example in learned multigrid prolongations [Greenfeld et al. (2019)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/greenfeld19a.html), [Luz et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/luz20a.html), machine-learning-assisted adaptive FETI-DP coarse spaces [Heinlein et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1344913) and networks trained through the solver [Um et al. (2020)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/43e4e6a6f341e00671e123714de019a8-Abstract.html), and hybrid neural solvers combine learned predictions with relaxation, learned inverse actions or coarse spaces [Zhang, E. et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42256-024-00910-x), [Kopaničáková & Karniadakis (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1137/24m162861x), [Xing et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3811333). Geometry-aware neural operators and graph networks [Li et al. (2023a)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v24/23-0064.html), [Li et al. (2023b)](https://doi.org/10.52202/075280-1556), [Pfaff et al. (2021)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=roNqYL0_XP), multigrid-structured operators [He et al. (2024)](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/eb3c8135137c8a60425a0320869ad87e-Abstract-Conference.html), learned Green's functions [Boullé & Townsend (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10208-022-09556-w) and training by energy minimisation [E & Yu (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40304-018-0127-z) instead learn the solution map of the whole problem.

From the viewpoint of algebraic multigrid, static condensation is a two-level method: the exact recovery \(\Psi\) is the ideal interpolation from the retained to all degrees of freedom, and \(S=\Psi^TK\Psi\) is the corresponding coarse-grid matrix [Falgout & Vassilevski (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1137/S0036142903429742). NICE approximates \(\Psi\) by a recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi\) predicted by a network and improved by a two-grid cycle, in the same spirit as smoothed-aggregation multigrid, which improves a tentative prolongation by smoothing [Vaněk et al. (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02238511). Like learned multigrid prolongations, \(\widehat\Psi\) is nonlinear in the cell geometry and linear in the retained displacements; unlike them, \(\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi\) is assembled into the lattice as a condensed stiffness, and the recovered displacement field is also used to compute the thickness sensitivities (Section 4.2). Compared with the work above, NICE keeps all retained degrees of freedom of a cell, its network takes the discrete geometry of each cut cell as input, and its correction acts on the condensed stiffness itself rather than only inside a preconditioner. Because the network output is linear in the retained displacements, the energy properties of static condensation hold for NICE as well. Unlike component reduced-basis methods, NICE provides no rigorous a posteriori error bound, only a computable interior residual (Eq. (7)).
