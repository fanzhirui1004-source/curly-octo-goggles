## 3. The NICE method

An approximate condensed stiffness \(\widehat S_m\) that replaces \(S_m\) in the design problem of Section 2.4 must meet three requirements. First, \(\widehat S_m\) must be symmetric positive semidefinite, with a null space containing only the rigid-body modes of the retained DOFs, and the supported assembled matrix \(\widehat{\mathbb K}\) must be positive definite, so that the assembled system can be solved. Second, the error of \(\widehat S_m\) must be measurable, and its effect on the compliance and the sensitivities quantifiable. Third, for a new geometry, including one whose discrete space is changed by the cut, applying \(\widehat S_m\) must not require a factorisation of the interior stiffness matrix.

NICE is defined by the corrected recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi\), which maps the retained displacements of a cell to the displacements of the whole cell; the condensed stiffness \(\widehat S=\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi\) follows from the strain energy of the recovered displacement field, and \(\widehat\Psi=\Psi\) gives exact static condensation. Section 3.1 computes the condensed stiffness from the strain energy and applies it with the transpose of \(\widehat\Psi\); for any recovery operator that reproduces the retained displacements and rigid-body motions, the condensed stiffness then meets the first requirement and is never softer than the exact one. For the second requirement, Section 4 relates the error of this matrix to the errors of the compliance and the sensitivities. The interior displacements are predicted by a network that takes the cell geometry as input, and the resulting recovery is exactly linear in the retained displacements and reproduces them by construction (Section 3.2). A fixed two-grid correction \(\mathcal W\) is applied to the predicted displacements before the strain energy is computed and reduces the interior force imbalance left by the network (Sections 3.3 and 3.4); the network is trained with this correction applied (Section 3.5). Neither the network nor the correction factorises the interior stiffness matrix, so the third requirement is met; the number of correction steps can also be increased at deployment without retraining. Section 3.6 describes how the condensed stiffness is applied and the assembled system solved, and Figure 2 shows how these parts define both the action of the condensed stiffness and the displacements recovered after assembly.

### 3.1. Condensed stiffness from the strain energy

The condensed stiffness is defined through the strain energy, under the original stiffness \(K\), of a recovered displacement field. Let \(\widehat\Psi\) be a linear recovery operator that maps the retained displacements \(q\) to the displacements \(\widehat\Psi q\) of the whole cell and reproduces the retained displacements, \(J_P\widehat\Psi=I_p\), and the rigid-body motions, \(\widehat\Psi R_P=R\), where \(R\) contains the rigid-body modes of Section 2.3. Propositions 1, 3 and 4 hold for any linear recovery operator with these two properties. In NICE, \(\widehat\Psi\) consists of the network recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}\) followed by the two-grid correction \(\mathcal W\), both described at the end of this section, and has these two properties by construction. The recovery operator and its condensed stiffness are

\[
\widehat\Psi=\mathcal W\widehat\Psi_{\rm net},\qquad
\widehat S=\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi,\qquad
\widehat{\mathcal U}(q)=\tfrac12q^T\widehat S q,\qquad
\widehat f_P=\widehat S q.
\tag{4}
\]

Here \(\widehat{\mathcal U}(q)\) is the strain energy of the recovered field, and \(\widehat f_P\) are the corresponding nodal forces on the retained DOFs. The definitions of \(\widehat S\), \(\widehat{\mathcal U}\) and \(\widehat f_P\) in Eq. (4) apply to any recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi\); only the relation \(\widehat\Psi=\mathcal W\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}\) is specific to NICE. The form \(\widehat S=\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi\) coincides with that of substructure methods in which the condensed stiffness matrix is computed from a matrix of numerical shape functions \(N\) as \(N^TKN\) [Huang et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eml.2023.102041); NICE, however, does not form \(\widehat\Psi\). For a virtual retained displacement \(\delta q\), the virtual displacement of the whole cell is \(\widehat\Psi\delta q\), and the variation of the strain energy is \(\delta\widehat{\mathcal U}=(\widehat\Psi\delta q)^TK\widehat\Psi q=\delta q^T\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi q\), so applying the condensed stiffness requires the transpose \(\widehat\Psi^T\) after the stiffness action. In NICE, this transpose includes every step of \(\widehat\Psi\): the linear displacement path of the network, the rigid-body reconstruction, the restoration of the retained displacements and the correction. With \(\widehat\Psi_I=J_I\widehat\Psi\), the action of the condensed stiffness has the block form

\[
\widehat S q=(K\widehat\Psi q)_P+\widehat\Psi_I^T(K\widehat\Psi q)_I.
\tag{5}
\]

The second term transfers the work of the interior residual \((K\widehat\Psi q)_I\), the interior force imbalance of the recovered field, to the retained DOFs; it vanishes when the recovered field is in interior equilibrium and is otherwise needed for the returned forces to be the derivative of the strain energy \(\widehat{\mathcal U}\). The symmetry and positive semidefiniteness of \(\widehat S\) follow from \(K=K^T\succeq0\) and do not require the scatter and gather maps, or the interpolation and restriction, of the network to be transposes of each other (Section 3.2). Since the null space of \(K\) consists of the rigid-body modes (Section 2.3) and \(\widehat\Psi R_P=R\), the only zero-energy modes of \(\widehat S\) are the retained rigid-body modes (Appendix B). The transpose \(\widehat\Psi^T\) is derived in Appendix E, and Appendix B.2 shows that, with this transpose, the error of the condensed stiffness is quadratic in the interior error.

By the principle of minimum potential energy, the exact field \(\Psi q\) has the smallest strain energy among all displacement fields with retained displacements \(q\). Any recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi\) with \(J_P\widehat\Psi=J_P\Psi=I_p\) differs from \(\Psi\) only in the interior DOFs; let \(H=J_I(\widehat\Psi-\Psi)\) denote this interior error. Because the exact field is in interior equilibrium, \(J_IK\Psi=0\), the cross terms between \(\Psi\) and \(H\) vanish from \(\widehat S=\widehat\Psi^TK\widehat\Psi\) (Appendix B.2).

**Proposition 1 (Ritz identity).** If a linear recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi\) satisfies \(J_P\widehat\Psi=I_p\) and \(\widehat\Psi R_P=R\), its condensed stiffness exceeds the exact condensed stiffness \(S\) by the energy of the interior error:

\[
\boxed{\widehat S-S=H^TAH\succeq0.}
\tag{6}
\]

An approximate recovery operator therefore adds stiffness equal to the energy of its interior error; the error has no first-order term, because the exact field is in interior equilibrium, and exact static condensation is the case \(\widehat\Psi=\Psi\), \(H=0\). For a given retained displacement \(q\), called a test displacement, the accuracy of \(\widehat S\) is measured by the relative energy error \(\varepsilon(q)=q^T(\widehat S-S)q/(q^TSq)\), which is nonnegative by Eq. (6). Let \(u=\Psi q\), \(\widehat u=\widehat\Psi q\) and \(d_I=Hq\); the interior residual \(r_I=(K\widehat u)_I\) satisfies \(r_I=Ad_I\), so that the energy error is

\[
\varepsilon(q)=\frac{d_I^TAd_I}{q^TSq}
=\frac{r_I^TA^{-1}r_I}{q^TSq}.
\tag{7}
\]

The residual can be computed without the exact recovery \(\Psi\), but evaluating its \(A^{-1}\)-norm requires one interior solve per test displacement, and a bound on the energy error over all retained displacements follows only from how well the sampled test displacements cover them (Appendices B and G.4). Proposition 1 and the residual form of Eq. (7) are classical results [Fraeijs de Veubeke (1965)](https://doi.org/10.1002/nme.339), [Toselli & Widlund (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1007/b137868), [Becker & Rannacher (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0962492901000010).

In NICE, \(\widehat\Psi=\mathcal W\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}\). Section 3.2 constructs the network recovery operator \(\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}\), which reproduces the retained displacements, \(J_P\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}=I_p\), and the rigid-body motions, \(\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}R_P=R\), by construction (Eq. (9)). The correction \(\mathcal W\) is one symmetric two-grid cycle applied with the retained displacements held fixed (Sections 3.3 and 3.4); it is linear and leaves the retained displacements unchanged, and taking \(\mathcal W\) as the identity gives the recovery without correction, \(\widehat\Psi=\widehat\Psi_{\rm net}\). For each geometry, the steps of the cycle and their coefficients are fixed independently of the retained displacements, and a correction driven by the interior residual, such as \(\mathcal W\), leaves a rigid-body field unchanged (Appendix B.2). The corrected operator \(\widehat\Psi\) thus also reproduces the retained displacements and the rigid-body motions, and Proposition 1 applies to it. By Eq. (6), the error of the condensed stiffness is the energy of the interior error of \(\widehat\Psi\), which Sections 3.3 and 3.4 reduce. After the assembled system is solved, \(\widehat\Psi_mB_m\widehat U\) gives the displacements of cell \(m\) for evaluating the response and the sensitivities.
