### 5.3. Accuracy on single cells

In every cut-severity group, the mean energy error of NICE is about 0.1% or less (Figure 5). Under traction loads, the group means of NICE are lower than those of the base network by factors of 71 to 117. The error of NICE still grows about sevenfold from uncut to heavily cut cells. Its group means rise from 0.016% for uncut to 0.109% for heavily cut cells, whereas those of the base network rise from 1.142% to 12.702% (Supplementary Table ST03b). Over all 80 geometries, the mean energy error is 0.074% for NICE and 6.89% for the base network. The largest single-geometry mean is 0.65% for NICE and 48.6% for the base network. Over the 5,120 sampled test displacements under traction loads, 99% of the energy errors of NICE lie below 0.69%, and the largest is 1.24% (Supplementary Table ST03).

Under nodal point loads, the mean energy error of the base network rises from 0.908% for uncut to 11.3% for heavily cut cells, and its largest single-geometry mean is 70.1%. The group means of NICE under these loads stay between 0.013% and 0.090%. Under the other load classes, the means of NICE are of the same order (Supplementary Table ST03). Figure S02 shows the distributions over the geometries. On the 60 geometries outside checkpoint selection, the class means of NICE differ from those over all 80 geometries by at most 0.005 percentage points. Under traction loads, the mean of NICE for the heavily cut group is higher on these 60 geometries, 0.126% against 0.109%. The five largest single-geometry means of NICE under traction loads all belong to these 60 geometries (Supplementary Table ST03).

Most of the improvement comes from the correction itself. Applying the correction to the base network at deployment, without retraining, lowers the mean under traction loads from 6.89% to 0.0965%. With the correction, the largest single-geometry mean of the base network is 0.91%. Continuing the training of the base network without the correction leaves its mean nearly unchanged (Supplementary Note S8). NICE adds further training through the correction and draws on a larger set of training geometries. This lowers the mean by a further factor of 1.31 (Supplementary Table ST03). The 95% bootstrap interval of this factor over the geometries is 1.20–1.40. Under the same correction, a deterministic graph-harmonic initial field leaves 5.4 to 265 times the error of NICE (Section 5.5).

### 5.4. Where the network error lies

Part of the error left by the network lies in modes that smoothing damps slowly, and part lies next to the cut. The error is also stiffer than the exact solution. This section examines the error before the correction in four respects. The first two are its distribution over the interior spectrum and its distribution in space. The third is the reason why an interior displacement error of about one percent produces an energy error of several percent. The fourth is how its effect on the sensitivity divides between the linear and the quadratic term of Eq. (17).

The error of the base network occupies different parts of the Jacobi-scaled interior spectrum in different cells (Figure 6). In M1 under traction loads, the lowest 200 modes contain 24.5% of the error energy but only 4.87% of the energy of the exact field (Supplementary Table ST05b). All 200 modes lie below the lower endpoint \(a=0.173\) of the smoothing interval, with \(a=b/30\) (Table 1). In H2, only 66 of the 200 modes lie below \(a=0.138\). Smoothing damps these modes slowly and leaves them to the coarse-grid correction (Section 5.5).

Part of the error of the base network lies next to the cut plane (Figure 7). In M1, the elements within two element widths of the cut plane make up 13% of the elements. For the traction-load test displacement plotted, the exact field places 8% of its energy in these elements. Over the four test displacements evaluated, 39–43% of the error energy of the base network lies in this layer. The layer adjoins the retained DOFs of the cut-plane elements, whose values the network must propagate into the interior. Relative to the base network, NICE reduces the total error energy by about two orders of magnitude, by factors of 113 to 138. It reduces the element error energies by factors of roughly 25 to 300 (10th to 90th percentiles). It also halves the fraction of the error energy in this layer, to 19–22%.

A small displacement error produces a large energy error when the error is stiff, and this amplification can be measured. Let \(d_I\) be the interior displacement error and \(u_I\) the exact interior displacements. Let \(\delta^2=d_I^TDd_I/u_I^TDu_I\) be the Jacobi-weighted relative displacement error. Let \(\kappa=(d_I^TAd_I/d_I^TDd_I)/(u^TKu/u_I^TDu_I)\) be the ratio of the Rayleigh quotients of the interior error and of the exact field. Then \(\varepsilon=\delta^2\kappa\) holds identically (Appendix B.3). Under traction loads, the base network has mean \(\delta\) of 0.7–1.3% in M1, M2, H1 and H2, but mean \(\kappa\) of 124 to 7089 (Supplementary Table ST04). The displacement error is therefore small, but relative to its amplitude it is far stiffer than the exact field. The resulting energy errors are 1.8–35%. In the uncut cell U2, a similar \(\delta\) of 0.74% combines with \(\kappa=85\) and gives an energy error of 0.40%. For NICE, \(\delta\) is 0.05–0.19% and \(\kappa\) is 33 to 582 in the cut cells, and the two values are 0.11% and 47 in U2.

Where the errors of the base network are large, the quadratic term of Eq. (17) dominates the sensitivity error. In M1 and H2, the sensitivity errors at fixed retained displacements are 14.1% and 75.1% under traction loads. The linear term contributes only 7.2% and 0.79% of the sum of the norms of the linear and quadratic terms. Under traction loads, the linear term contributes 28–43% in the three cells whose energy errors are below 2%, namely U1, U2 and H1. Under nodal point loads, it contributes 24–72% in all six cells. Because the linear term is first order in the displacement error, it dominates as this error tends to zero (Supplementary Table ST05 and Figure S03).

### 5.5. Correction of a fixed network

With the base network held fixed, the effect of the correction can be examined in isolation (Figure 8). Eight Chebyshev smoothing steps at fixed retained displacements reduce the mean energy error in every cell, but unevenly (Supplementary Table ST05a). Under traction loads, the error of H2 falls from 35.0% to 0.205%. The error of M1 falls only from 13.5% to 4.69%, and to 2.95% after 32 steps, because much of it lies in modes below the smoothing interval (Section 5.4). The sensitivity error of M1 falls from 14.1% to 2.76%. In U1, the energy error decreases at every step, whereas the sensitivity error varies non-monotonically with the number of steps (Figure 8a,b).

A trilinear coarse-grid correction applied before the same eight steps reduces the error of M1 to 0.230%. Eight additional pre-smoothing steps reduce it further to 0.186%. The coarse space of M1 has 5,601 DOFs for 165,927 interior DOFs. With two, four and eight steps per smoothing stage, the two-grid cycle gives 1.02%, 0.422% and 0.186% on M1. The eight-step cycle gives about 0.027% on M2 and U1 (Supplementary Table ST07). Supplementary Table ST06 compares other coarse spaces.

Table 4 applies the same correction to four initial fields at the same retained displacements. These are zero interior displacements, a graph-harmonic initial field, the field of the base network and the field of NICE. The graph-harmonic field is computed with a volume-weighted graph Laplacian on the element connectivity, after the same exact separation of the rigid-body part as for the network. After the correction, zero interior displacements leave energy errors of 28–1100%, and the graph-harmonic field leaves 0.08–17%. The field of the base network ends at 0.004–0.19%. This is lower by factors of 7 to 290 than the graph-harmonic field and by factors of 2,400 to 12,000 than the zero field. Even with 64 steps per stage, eight times the smoothing work, the graph-harmonic field remains 2.6 to 50 times above the eight-step result of the base network. This holds under traction loads in five of the six cells of Supplementary Table ST07, which are the cells of Table 4 and U1. Only in H2 does the graph-harmonic field reach the result of the base network. The network prediction therefore accounts for the part of the interior equilibrium that neither the smoothing nor the coarse space reaches.
